# Courtyard textured mesh study

This is an actual 3D section developed from the approved courtyard concept: a staggered broken wall, worn paving, continuous soil, rubble and clustered foliage. It is an art study, not the full courtyard and not production gameplay integration. Open `project.godot` in Godot 4.6, import, and run; A/D orbit and W/S zoom.

The GLB includes five material groups with UVs and vertex colors. Limestone and soil use separate embedded AI-generated base-color images with authored vertex tints. Leaf and flower colors are authored vertex colors. Geometry is generated in Blender 4.5.9: run `source/build.py`, then `source/refine_soil.py` once to apply the soil material, then `source/fix_contacts.py` to resolve the rubble contact. The `.blend` is editable and packs both textures. Source and review directories are excluded from Godot import.

The source is Z-up in metres; GLB is Y-up. The section occupies roughly 6 by 4.8 metres. There is no per-cell mesh arrangement. The scene has no collision, navigation, combat, LODs or character integration. NOVA should continue to use TacticalGrid as gameplay authority and the existing presentation height conversion when integrating a later approved version.

Review images are real Blender renders, not generated concept imagery. The original approved concept is in the sibling `courtyard-art-target` output folder. The generated limestone texture is a base-color study, not a measured PBR material set; its tiling and long-range repetition have not been approved. It was generated with the built-in image tool. Stylized painterly fantasy masonry for an Arcane / No Rest for the Wicked inspired ruined garden, crafted restrained surface detail. Flat even diffuse illumination, no light source, no cast shadows, no ambient occlusion, no perspective, no objects, no masonry grid, no individual bricks, no borders, no text. Midtone limestone, not bright white. Low contrast larger patches and sparse darker weathered cracks; no photoreal noise."

Review the actual geometry and texture density at gameplay size before expanding the kit. Material richness, distinct masonry silhouettes and natural plant clustering matter more than asset count. QA notes: material groups contain intentionally separate stones/plants. UVs stack for repeating material textures, with export vertices split at seams; these are not unique bake UVs. Below-zero geometry embeds soil, foliage roots and paving around the walking surface. Hidden contact faces within leaf clusters and masonry remain an optimization task. Small foliage uses 0.2 mm coplanarity tolerance; masonry uses 1 mm. Review JSON records the full checks. No browser performance or manual gameplay validation is claimed.

Final export step: after the three build/refinement scripts, run `source/export_final.py` to triangulate, clean tiny degenerate edges, recalculate normals and explicitly export active vertex colors. Then run `source/verify.py` and `source/render_export.py`. The latter generates `review/export-roundtrip.png` from the exported GLB; use this as the most authoritative appearance check. `section.png`, `reverse.png` and `wire.png` document the preceding source review.

Validation: Godot 4.6.2 headless import and scene-instantiation checks pass. Each of five material batches passes the geometry checks after welding export seam duplicates on inspection copies. Raw material-batch separation findings remain in the report: individual leaves and stems are deliberately separate throughout this environment and must not be interpreted as a single connected prop. This does not establish full-scene contact correctness or production readiness. The local offline Godot run emits a certificate-store warning. Full-scene collision and performance are untested.
